### Page 1 — What Is a Freedom Number?
**Headline:** One Number Replaces All the Vague Financial Goals

**Content:**
- **Definition:** "Your Freedom Number is the monthly passive income your investments need to generate to cover your living expenses. When your money earns more than you spend, working becomes a choice, not a necessity."
- **Why this matters in Singapore:** CPF exists, but CPF LIFE at FRS only pays ~$1,600/month — well below what most professionals need. The gap must be filled by personal investment income.
- **The formula preview:**

```
Freedom Number = Monthly Expenses − Guaranteed Income Sources
```

- **What it is NOT:** A guarantee. A get-rich-quick promise. A product pitch.

### Page 2 — Singapore Reality Check
**Headline:** Why Generic Financial Advice Doesn't Work Here

**Content:**
- Key stats:
  - Singapore ranked most expensive city globally 8 of last 10 years (EIU)
  - 60% of workers living paycheck-to-paycheck in 2024 (ADP)
  - HDB resale prices up 116% since 2000 (SmartWealth)
  - Education costs up 74.7% since 2003 (SmartWealth)

- **The trap:** "If your income grew 40% but your key expenses grew 75%, you've quietly gone backwards — even with a good salary."

- **What makes Singapore unique for this calculation:**
  1. CPF provides a retirement floor — but only partially covers needs
  2. HDB equity can be a retirement asset — but locks up capital
  3. SRS offers tax-efficient savings — but most people don't use it
  4. Your Freedom Number must account for all of these, not ignore them

### Page 5 — Step 3: Your Freedom Number
**Headline:** The Calculation

**Content:**

**The Formula:**
```
FREEDOM NUMBER = Monthly Retirement Expenses − Guaranteed Income
```

**Example:**
```
Monthly retirement target:     S$6,000
CPF LIFE (FRS):               −S$1,600
Rental income:                 −S$1,200
─────────────────────────────────────
Freedom Number:                 S$3,200/month
```

**What this means:**
> "You need your investment portfolio to generate S$3,200/month in passive income. At a 4% sustainable withdrawal rate, that requires an investment portfolio of approximately S$960,000."

### Page 7 — The 3 Levers Singapore Professionals Use
**Headline:** How Do You Close the Gap?

**Content:**

**Lever 1: Increase Monthly Savings**
- The most direct lever
- Where to invest: SRS (for tax relief), unit trusts/ETFs, CPFIS (for CPF OA), direct equity
- Tax angle: SRS contributions reduce taxable income dollar-for-dollar. At 15% tax bracket, a S$15,300 SRS contribution saves S$2,295 in taxes annually.
- "Small increases compound significantly: S$300/month more, invested at 5% for 20 years = ~S$123,000 extra at retirement."

**Lever 2: Improve Investment Returns**
- Moving from cash savings (0.05%) to a diversified portfolio (4–6%) can shorten your timeline by years
- Singapore-relevant options:
  - Unit trusts / ETFs (Endowus, Syfe, StashAway platforms available in SG)
  - REITs (local SGX-listed, dividend-focused)
  - CPFIS (OA funds invested in equities/bonds)
  - Regular premium investment-linked plans
- Caution: Do not chase returns beyond your risk tolerance or timeline

**Lever 3: Reduce the Freedom Number**
- Downsize housing in retirement (move to smaller HDB, generate cash)
- Pay off mortgage before retirement to eliminate that expense
- Develop part-time income in retirement (consulting, tutoring)
- Maximise CPF voluntary top-ups now to increase guaranteed CPF LIFE payout (reducing your Freedom Number)

**Summary:**
> "You don't need to pull all three levers at maximum force. Most people find a combination that fits their cash flow and risk profile. That's what a financial plan maps out."
