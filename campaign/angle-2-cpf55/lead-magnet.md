### Page 1 — What Changed in 2025–2026 (Plain English)

**Section Title:** "The 4 CPF Changes That Affect You in the Next 3 Years"

**Change 1: Special Account (SA) Closes at 55**
> From 19 January 2025, CPF closes the Special Account for members aged 55 and above. Your SA balance is transferred: first into your Retirement Account (RA) up to the FRS, then any excess goes to your Ordinary Account (OA).
> **What this means:** The SA's 4% interest rate no longer accumulates separately. Your RA earns 4%, OA earns 2.5%. If you had excess SA funds, they now sit in OA at lower interest.

**Change 2: Monthly Salary Ceiling Rising to S$8,000**
> CPF monthly salary ceiling is increasing in stages: S$6,300 (Sep 2023) → S$6,800 (Jan 2024) → S$7,400 (Jan 2025) → S$8,000 (Jan 2026).
> **What this means:** If you earn above these amounts, more of your salary now attracts CPF contributions — which can boost your RA balance before retirement.

**Change 3: Contribution Rates for Workers Above 55 Are Rising**
> From Jan 2025: Total contribution rates for workers aged 55–65 increased by 1.5 percentage points (0.5% employer + 1% employee), with a further 1.5-point increase in 2026.
> **What this means:** More is flowing into your CPF — good for retirement savings, but slightly reduces take-home pay.

**Change 4: Enhanced Retirement Sum (ERS) Raised to 4× BRS**
> From 2025, the ERS is raised from 3× to 4× the Basic Retirement Sum. You can now top up to S$440,800 (2026 ERS) to unlock higher CPF LIFE payouts of approximately S$3,180–3,410/month.
> **What this means:** If you have the means, topping up to ERS now offers a meaningful income boost in retirement.

### Page 3 — Your Personal CPF 55 Checklist

**Section Title:** "What to Do Before You Turn 55 (and After)"

**Before 55 (if you're 50–54):**
- [ ] Check your CPF SA balance now — it will move to RA at 55
- [ ] Consider voluntary top-ups to SA (to earn 4% before closure) — deadline is before your 55th birthday
- [ ] Review your Retirement Account target: BRS / FRS / ERS — which makes sense for you?
- [ ] Check if you've set aside for your spouse (spousal top-up may qualify for tax relief)
- [ ] Estimate your monthly expenses in retirement — do you know your number?

**At 55:**
- [ ] Understand your withdrawal options — you can withdraw S$5,000 unconditionally, plus any CPF above your FRS (or above BRS if you own property)
- [ ] Choose your CPF LIFE plan: Standard Plan (higher payouts) vs Escalating Plan (lower start, increases 2% annually)
- [ ] Review how much cash/investment you have OUTSIDE CPF — this is your retirement income gap to fill

**After 55 (before 65):**
- [ ] Review your investments annually — are they positioned to generate income by 65?
- [ ] Consider whether to top up to ERS before 65 if cashflow allows (4% interest)
- [ ] Ensure you have a written retirement income plan that covers all sources: CPF LIFE + investments + property rental (if applicable) + business income

### Page 4 — The Retirement Income Gap (Your To-Do)

**Section Title:** "Where Does the Other 50% Come From?"

**Key data:**
> "For a typical middle-income retiree in Singapore, CPF LIFE covers an estimated 20–50% of total retirement spending. The rest — often S$1,500–2,000/month — must come from personal savings, investments, or other income sources."
> — StashAway 2026 Retirement Analysis

**The Gap Formula:**
```
Your desired monthly retirement income
MINUS CPF LIFE projected payout
= Your Monthly Gap to Fill
```

Example:
```
Desired: S$3,500/month
CPF LIFE (FRS): S$1,600/month
Gap: S$1,900/month
To fund 20 years: S$1,900 × 12 × 20 = S$456,000 needed outside CPF
```
